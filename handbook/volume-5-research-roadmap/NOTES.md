# Volume 5 — Parking Lot

## Long-term continuity (corrected framing, 2026-08-01)
See `../founding-problem.md`. Earlier draft of this note over-read the
"10,000–20,000 years" language as a literal civilizational-engineering
target (seed-vault/Long-Now-style institutional permanence, legal trust
succession, etc.). Corrected: the actual requirement is that the system
keep telling people's stories — where they were born, what happened to
them, what they carried — for as long as the application itself stays
alive. That's an ordinary (if serious) durability problem, not an
institutional one:

- **Data**: non-proprietary, self-describing storage; no silent deletion;
  standard backup and migration discipline as formats age.
- **No new institution required.** Don't design around the assumption the
  company won't exist — design around the product staying maintained and
  the data staying portable if it ever needs to move.
- **Evidence discipline still compounds over time**: an error introduced
  once and carried forward for years is worse than one caught early. The
  Evidence Hierarchy (Volume 1, Ch. 6) is the real safeguard here, more
  than any storage architecture.

Kept lightweight deliberately — this is a durability requirement on an
ongoing product, not a research problem in civilizational preservation.
